## Strongest case against H003

1. **Stable medium-term inflation expectations:** the New York Fed's August consumer survey showed five-year inflation expectations steady at 3.0% and three-year expectations edging down to 3.2%. A large oil shock has therefore not yet produced evidence of medium-term expectation unanchoring.
2. **Growth-negative alternative:** the same survey showed sharply worse unemployment expectations, weaker job-finding confidence and deteriorating household financial/credit views. Persistent energy costs can compress real purchasing power and shift the dominant consequence toward H002 rather than H003.
3. **Endpoint ambiguity:** the Sep. 8 10-year yield at 4.80% and S&P decline are downstream market outcomes influenced by oil, Fed expectations, AI/software repricing, positioning and other factors. They cannot uniquely validate H003.
4. **Timing:** the Sep. 5-8 oil escalation occurred after the August CPI/PPI reference period and cannot be used to explain or raise confidence in already-realized August inflation data.
5. **Forecast-error discipline:** P000007's WTI outcome above its frozen 80% interval proves that the Aug. 8 commodity distribution missed a realized tail. It does not prove the broader inflation-regime mechanism; one 80% miss is compatible with a calibrated interval process.

## Hidden assumptions in an H003 upgrade
- that a spot/settlement oil shock passes materially into underlying inflation rather than mostly headline energy;
- that pass-through is large enough and persistent enough to alter the Fed reaction function;
- that stable medium-term expectations will not remain anchored;
- that higher yields are mainly inflation-policy effects rather than term premium, supply, growth or positioning;
- that the energy shock will not become sufficiently growth-negative to favor H002.

## Discriminators
- Sep. 10 first-release August PPI: upstream producer-price pipeline, with the September oil shock explicitly outside the reference window.
- Sep. 11 first-release August core CPI: cleaner H001-vs-H003 underlying-inflation discriminator.
- Later consumer/labor data: distinguishes an inflation-dominant energy shock from a real-income/growth-damage pathway.

## Skeptic conclusion
Do **not** raise H003 today. Preserve the physical energy-risk evidence and the P000007 tail miss, but keep weights unchanged until producer/consumer inflation or subsequent real-demand evidence discriminates among the competing pathways. Do not widen intervals or add a new causal edge solely because one 80% WTI interval missed.
