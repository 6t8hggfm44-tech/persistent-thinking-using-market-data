# Skeptic — 2026-10-02

**Target before update:** H003 — fiscal / inflation regime, weight 0.39.

The strongest contrary evidence is direct rather than market-implied: first-release September payroll growth was only +29,000, unemployment rose to 4.2%, average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% m/m and 3.0% y/y, and July-August payrolls were revised down by a combined 60,000. Those observations weaken a story in which labor demand itself is maintaining broad inflation pressure and forcing near-term policy tightening.

A serious H001/H002 alternative is a cooling labor market with unusually low layoffs: employers are hiring less without yet conducting mass layoffs. The October 1 claims data therefore cannot be used as a sufficient statistic for labor demand. Likewise, the post-jobs rally and lower rate-hike odds are downstream endpoints and do not independently validate a soft landing.

The strongest defense of H003 is that the jobs report does not measure inflation directly. September ISM prices remained very high, earlier activity indicators were resilient, energy/supply risks remain unresolved, and one payroll month is noisy and revision-prone. A late Labor Day may also have depressed the first September payroll estimate. Unemployment remains low in absolute terms and the participation increase is not a classic layoff shock.

**Discriminator:** the first-release September CPI on October 14 is now more informative about H003 than another market-price reaction. A headline CPI print below +0.4% m/m, especially with continued soft labor data, would further weaken the near-term inflation/policy-constraint branch. A print at or above +0.4% while labor remains low-layoff would preserve a stagflationary / supply-pressure version of H003 even after the payroll slowdown.

**Conclusion:** H003 should lose weight, but not collapse. The labor evidence transfers some mass to H002 and a smaller amount to H001; H004 is not implicated.
