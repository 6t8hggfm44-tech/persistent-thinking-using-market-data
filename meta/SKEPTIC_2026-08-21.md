# Skeptic Review — 2026-08-21

**Leading hypothesis before review:** H001 Soft Landing, weight 0.35  
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-08-21T17:24:57-04:00

## Strongest case against H001

The strongest new activity evidence is not uniquely a soft-landing signal. August flash services PMI at 56.8 and composite output at 56.0 show robust demand, but price pressures remain elevated while Brent is $94.39, WTI $87.06, and long Treasury yields have rebounded to roughly 4.73% / 5.266% on the 10-/30-year. If strong demand and energy pressure keep policy expectations and term premium elevated, the same growth data can favor H003 over H001: the economy is resilient **because nominal demand is strong enough to keep the inflation/rate constraint binding**, not because disinflation has safely completed.

A second problem is breadth. P000019 failed because the model over-weighted very strong Empire State and Philadelphia headline surveys: national flash manufacturing PMI was only 53.2, below July and below both frozen point benchmarks. Services, not factories, drove the new composite acceleration. H001 therefore cannot simply translate every strong activity headline into broad benign expansion.

A third problem is financial conditions. Treasury's long-end buyback produced only brief relief before yields rebounded. At the same time, AI-hyperscaler bond issuance has become large enough that investors are demanding wider spreads and larger concessions. This creates a route by which strong investment demand itself raises financing costs and eventually restrains the expansion.

## Hidden assumptions in H001

1. The current energy shock will not materially reaccelerate inflation expectations or later measured prices.
2. Strong services demand can coexist with sufficient disinflation to avoid a renewed tightening cycle.
3. The low-layoff labor regime will eventually stabilize hiring/re-employment rather than merely postpone labor deterioration.
4. Treasury debt-management and other liquidity tools can cushion long-end stress enough to prevent a persistent financial-conditions shock.
5. Fresh credit evidence, which was not verified today, will continue to resemble the previously tight-spread regime.

## Best alternative explanation

**H003 Fiscal / Inflation Regime:** resilient nominal demand, energy disruption, public and private duration supply, and a less-accommodative policy reaction function jointly keep long yields and financing costs elevated even without recession. Under this explanation, strong services activity is not evidence against the problem; it is part of the reason the policy constraint persists.

## What would discriminate H001 from H003

- Strong activity accompanied by falling inflation expectations, easing core inflation, and sustainably lower long yields favors H001.
- Strong activity accompanied by firmer inflation expectations, persistent energy pass-through, higher policy expectations, and elevated long yields favors H003.
- Because the August oil shock postdates July price data, later August/September inflation expectations and price releases are more informative than July PCE alone for the energy-pass-through branch.

## What would discriminate H001 from H002

The newly separated labor-flow model should be tested jointly: low initial claims with sticky/rising continued claims would support a low-fire / weak-reemployment regime rather than either a clean labor boom or a layoff-led recession. A simultaneous jump in initial claims and continued claims would be much stronger H002 confirmation.

## Skeptic conclusion before model update

H001 remains plausible and today's broad activity data weaken H002, but H001 should no longer be the automatic beneficiary of strong growth prints. Given elevated energy, a rebound in long yields, and direct confirmation that private AI-duration supply is becoming costly to absorb, H003 now has a serious claim to the lead. The update should be modest because later inflation pass-through and fresh credit confirmation are still missing.
