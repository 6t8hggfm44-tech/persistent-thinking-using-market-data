## Strongest case against H003

1. **P000023 was a threshold hit, not an extreme inflation surprise.** Core CPI was 0.3% m/m, exactly the precommitted event boundary. The forecast assigned only 54% to the event. This is modest positive evidence, not a large likelihood shock.
2. **Core year-over-year inflation eased to 2.4%.** A 0.3% monthly print can coexist with a broader disinflation trend; it does not prove a durable inflation regime.
3. **The headline acceleration was materially energy-linked.** Energy rose 2.1% and gasoline 3.9%. A geopolitical energy shock can raise headline inflation while simultaneously reducing real household purchasing power and future demand.
4. **Consumer sentiment weakened sharply.** Preliminary September sentiment fell to 47.8 and expectations to 45.8. That supports a demand-side slowdown rival even while household inflation expectations rose.
5. **Oil fell on Friday after Thursday's surge.** Brent and WTI remained very high but declined on the day, showing that the current shock path is volatile and sensitive to conflict/news flow rather than a stable monotonic state.
6. **Market prices are downstream endpoints.** Nearly 90% implied hike odds, a near-5% 10-year yield, and an equity rally are mutually compatible with several narratives. They are not independent causal confirmations because they react to the same CPI/energy/news state.

## Hidden assumptions in H003

- That the energy shock will persist long enough to pass through into broader core prices rather than primarily reduce real demand.
- That the Fed will place more weight on near-term inflation persistence than on weakening household sentiment or later activity deterioration.
- That consumer inflation expectations are a causal persistence mechanism rather than largely a contemporaneous response to gasoline prices.
- That resilient payrolls and low claims imply enough demand strength to absorb higher energy/rate costs without a later contraction.

## Alternative explanations

- **H001 soft landing:** core y/y inflation at 2.4% may still be compatible with disinflation even if one monthly print is 0.3%; resilient labor can coexist with a temporary energy-driven headline shock.
- **H002 delayed recession:** high energy prices and near-5% long yields may tighten real household and corporate conditions enough to create a delayed downturn; weak sentiment is consistent with this pathway.
- **H004 productivity boom:** strong AI/capex earnings can support equities and activity despite restrictive rates, but there is still no direct economy-wide productivity discriminator.

## Discriminating observations

- P000027's Sep. 16 FOMC decision is the next frozen direct test of the policy-reaction mapping. It must be scored under its original 58% probability without using today's near-90% market odds to rewrite it.
- Persistent core inflation in subsequent releases without a corresponding collapse in real demand would strengthen H003 more than today's threshold hit.
- A combination of still-high energy/long yields with rising layoffs, widening credit, or materially weaker spending would instead strengthen H002.

## Skeptic conclusion

H003 remains the narrow leader, but today's evidence does **not** justify a structural causal-graph change or a material weight increase. The P000023 success is real and preserved, yet it is modest, threshold-adjacent, and causally entangled with an energy shock that has both inflationary and contractionary channels. Keep H001/H002 alternatives alive and let P000027 provide the next precommitted discrimination.
