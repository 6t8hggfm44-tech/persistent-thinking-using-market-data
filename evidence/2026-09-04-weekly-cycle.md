## E-2026-09-04-01 — August Employment Situation

**Available:** 2026-09-04 08:30 ET  
**Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, first August 2026 Employment Situation release  
**URL:** https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm

**OBSERVATION:** Total nonfarm payroll employment increased **162,000** in August and the unemployment rate was unchanged at **4.1%**. June payroll growth was revised from +20K to +31K and July from -23K to +21K, a combined upward revision of **55K**. Labor-force participation rose 0.2 pp to **61.6%** and household employment rose 569K. Average hourly earnings rose **0.3% m/m and 3.1% y/y**; the private workweek rose 0.1 hour to 34.4. Payroll gains were concentrated in food services/drinking places (+59K) and local-government education (+42K), while information lost 23K.

**INFERENCE:** The release is strong contrary evidence to the near-term generalized labor-deterioration branch and to the prior 38K payroll point estimate. It does not establish an economy-wide boom: sector concentration, a still-low participation rate relative to a year ago, and the distinction between household/CES measures remain relevant. Wage growth at 3.1% y/y is materially less inflationary than the payroll headline alone.

**Expected under hypotheses:**
- **H001:** more expected than under H002; resilient payrolls/stable unemployment fit a soft-landing labor path, though the strength is larger than the model expected.
- **H002:** surprising in the near term; requires a delayed-downturn interpretation rather than a current generalized contraction.
- **H003:** compatible because resilient labor can keep the policy constraint active, but wage moderation means the report is not uniquely inflationary.
- **H004:** weakly compatible but not discriminating; employment growth does not measure productivity.

**OBSERVATION:** Reuters reported Fed-funds futures pricing roughly a **61%** probability of a rate hike at the September meeting, up from **52%** before the jobs report. Citi moved its next expected Fed cut to June 2027.

**INFERENCE:** The market repricing is evidence about the market's reaction function estimate, not direct evidence that the Fed will hike or that H003 is true. It provides a timestamped external benchmark for a prospective policy forecast.

**Expected under hypotheses:**
- **H001:** compatible if the Fed can tighten modestly without causing contraction, but less benign than a disinflation-led hold/easing path.
- **H002:** contrary to an immediate recession-driven easing story.
- **H003:** supportive of a still-binding policy constraint, especially jointly with the labor and energy observations.
- **H004:** not strongly discriminating.
