# Labor-market activity and pressure — October 5, 2026

## Summary

**INFERENCE — Labor demand looks softer, but this evidence does not establish broad contraction or an emerging layoff surge.** September payroll growth was small, and revisions lowered the preceding two months. Unchanged private-sector hours and mixed turnover indicators limit the interpretation. The strongest new result is the revision-sensitive payroll comparison; the strongest caution is that the different surveys do not describe one common week.

This is a bounded final hypothesis test with explicit coverage gaps. No recession probability, causal attribution, investment recommendation or calibrated significance claim is assigned. Earlier Labor inputs were already known; tests are exploratory, with comparison rules declared before the new releases were read.

## Hypotheses and tests

**H1: Broad labor demand is weakening — partly supported by payroll softness; broad contraction remains unproven.**

OBSERVATION: September nonfarm payrolls rose **29,000**. Revised July and August changes were **−10,000** and **+133,000**. Their three-month sum is **152,000**, or about **50,667 jobs per month**. The October release lowered July and August by **60,000 combined**. Keeping their former estimates with September would yield about **70,667 per month**: a sensitivity calculation, not an actual historical three-month vintage.

The prior retained June–August mean was about **71,333**. Of the approximately **20,667** decline in the rolling mean, **20,000** comes from the two revisions and about **667** from replacing June with September. It would therefore be misleading to attribute the entire difference to September activity.

Private all-employee hours remained **34.4**; manufacturing hours and overtime were unchanged at **40.6** and **3.0**. These oppose the predeclared falling-hours prediction. The six-sector breadth count and employment-times-hours proxy are **blocked**: compatible complete sector changes and private payroll levels were not obtained. Counts cannot mix total nonfarm jobs with private-sector hours.

**H2: Weak hiring pressure need not imply a layoff surge — mixed, with no demonstrated surge in the measured periods.**

OBSERVATION: August JOLTS openings were **7.1 million**, hires **5.2 million**, quits **3.1 million**, and layoffs/discharges **1.6 million**. Subtracting revised July rounded counts gives approximately **−0.2 million** openings, **+0.1 million** hires, **zero** quits change and **−0.1 million** layoffs. BLS describes these as little changed or unchanged. The small rounded differences do not establish statistical significance. Rising hires weakens a simple across-the-board hiring decline story; lower openings alone is insufficient to establish it.

The openings-to-unemployed ratio is **blocked**: August household unemployment was not retained. September unemployed persons cannot be substituted. Openings are an end-of-month stock; hires and separations are monthly flows. Survey definitions and rate denominators remain distinct.

OBSERVATION from the older claims vintage: September 19 initial claims were **197,000**, versus revised **198,000**, a **1,000 decline**. Using the superseded **196,000** would falsely turn that into a **1,000 increase**. The published four-week average fell **1,750**, from **204,000** to **202,250**; it was not independently reconstructed because four compatible weekly inputs are absent. Continuing claims rose **2,000**, to **1,719,000**, for the earlier week ending September 12. Their preceding estimate was revised down **13,000**. Mixed directions and different weeks cannot prove current persistence or its absence.

**H3: Pay pressure has eased over the annual horizon — supported for the retained ECI comparison, with a quarterly qualification.**

OBSERVATION: Second-quarter civilian ECI compensation grew **3.4% over a year**, compared with **3.6%** in the year-earlier comparison: **−0.2 percentage point**. Wage growth fell from **3.6% to 3.2%**, or **−0.4 point**. Quarterly compensation growth remained **0.9%**, so the evidence does not show quarter-to-quarter deceleration. These figures retain their quarterly horizon and composition-controlled universe; they are not combined with monthly average earnings into a wage score.

**Executed checks:** revised-baseline claims differences, payroll sum/mean and revision decomposition, rounded same-vintage JOLTS differences, and ECI growth-rate differences. Decimal calculations were independently checked using rational arithmetic and the publisher's combined-revision identity. Central fresh inputs were re-read against their release locations. This checks arithmetic and transcription, not independent publisher-body identity or a second survey replication.

**Controls and contrary evidence:** revisions change the payroll magnitude and claims sign; hours are stable; hires rose at displayed precision; continuing claims rose while initial claims fell; quarterly compensation growth is unchanged despite annual easing. Survey noise, composition, participation, strike/weather effects and claims administration remain competing explanations. No eligible causal control design was available to rank those mechanisms.

## Limitations

ASSUMPTION: Retained reviewed cells accurately reflect their original dated source representations. Their exact saved bytes were verified, but the missing original bodies cannot be reread. Fresh tool text is also a derived representation. Upstream HTTP request, byte, redirect and duration totals were unmeasured under the authorized bounded access route.

Current claims acquisition was unavailable under its preserved source interval; quarterly ECI was reused to avoid duplicate evidence. Neither choice establishes latest-release availability. A source's scheduled frequency is not permission to interpolate missing periods. Detailed industry, temporary-help, payroll-hours, same-month tightness and claims-persistence tests remain blocked. Link labels alone were not treated as verified table URLs or table observations.

Sampling errors and confidence intervals were not acquired, so small differences cannot be declared significant. Industry estimates would also be correlated, not six independent confirmations. ECI, CES, CPS, JOLTS and UI cover different populations and dates. Source reuse across consumer, industry or market reports adds no independent evidence weight. No counterpart conclusions or market reactions were used to select or validate these findings.

INFERENCE: Slower payroll gains could restrain household income growth and hiring-sensitive activity, but stable hours and non-surging measured layoffs limit a stronger transmission claim. The lag, exposure and realized spending effects were not tested. SPECULATION: A broader downturn could emerge if future compatible hours, industry and claims observations deteriorate; this is a falsifiable possibility, not a finding.
