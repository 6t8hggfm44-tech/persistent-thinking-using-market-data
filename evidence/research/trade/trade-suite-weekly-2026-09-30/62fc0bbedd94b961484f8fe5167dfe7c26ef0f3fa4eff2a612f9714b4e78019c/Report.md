# Global trade and China — September 30, 2026

## Summary

**Final evidence-gap finding:** the available evidence supports a local container-composition result, but cannot establish a new global trade turning point. A fresh retrieval confirms the same August port observations previously reviewed. Current access to an explanatory European page does not supply current European production measurements.

## Hypotheses and tests

The plan was exploratory: the prior port figures and earlier conversation context were already known. Hypotheses, falsifiers, comparison periods and formulas were recorded before new collection and subsequent calculations.

**H1 — A flat aggregate masks weaker loaded activity offset by empties. Supported descriptively.** T1 recomputed each growth rate; T2 reconciled imports plus exports to loaded flows and loaded plus empty flows to totals, with zero residual in both years. Empty growth offset 84.72% of the loaded decline. Loaded share fell 1.51 percentage points. These accounting results fit H1; they are not a statistical significance or causal test.

**H2 — Local weakness reflects broad global real-demand contraction. Unresolved.** T3 requires other ports, compatible customs quantities, pre-event trends and defensible shipment lags. Those inputs are absent. T4 executed a temporal-eligibility check and rejected the retrieved European overview as current corroboration. No current European production comparison was executed.

**H3 — Asian export growth represents broad quantity demand rather than semiconductor price or product mix. Untested.** T5 lacks comparable complete-month Korean and Taiwanese tables, working-day controls, commodity classifications and quantity or unit-value measures. No partial-month extrapolation or nominal-to-real conversion was substituted.

**China and commodities:** T6 remains blocked. Compatible physical production, property, energy and commodity-import quantities were not available for testing. Existing acquisition uncertainty was preserved. No conclusion about Chinese activity follows from that absence.

## Interpretation and competing explanations

**ASSUMPTION:** same-calendar-month comparisons provide a useful descriptive baseline. They do not eliminate working-day changes, base effects or policy-related shipment timing.

Loaded movements can reflect customer demand, inventory placement, routing or operating capacity. Empty movements can reflect equipment repositioning. The directions are compatible with several mechanisms; distinguishing them requires contemporaneous exposure and control measurements. A port-volume change alone cannot establish manufacturer revenues, consumer expenditure, inflation or company earnings.

**SPECULATION, not adopted:** tariff front-loading or route substitution could explain part of the divergence. Neither mechanism was measured here. No market forecast or portfolio action is derived from this report.

## Limitations

The retained sample covers one matched month at one port. It cannot estimate trend persistence, uncertainty bands or national/global breadth. No cargo mix, alternate-port, seasonal-adjusted, customs-volume or tariff-timing controls were available. European coverage is explanatory and historically dated. China, Korea, Taiwan and commodity measurement gaps remain material.

Only semantically reviewed limited extracts and calculations are retained. Their exact hashes establish retained-content identity, not original publisher-body identity. Hidden transport requests, bytes and redirects are unmeasured. Previous source failures and acquisition uncertainty remain distinct from the successful page retrievals.

Decimal calculations were checked using independently implemented exact rational arithmetic. This verifies arithmetic on the same input cells; it does not provide independent source confirmation. Shared port observations count once across reports. No other domain’s final report was used to manufacture corroboration.
