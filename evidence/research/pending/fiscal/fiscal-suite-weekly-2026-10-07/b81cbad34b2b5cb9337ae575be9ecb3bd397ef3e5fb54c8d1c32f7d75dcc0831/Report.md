# Fiscal activity — October 7, 2026

## Summary

**Verdict: modest nominal public-construction resilience, but no statistically established broad acceleration and no verified new federal fiscal impulse.**

OBSERVATION: the October 1 Census release added August 2026. Total construction was $2,203.129 billion at a seasonally adjusted annual rate (SAAR), up 0.854% from revised July but down 1.735% from August 2025. Private construction rose 1.074% month over month and public construction rose 0.194%. The reported 90% sampling intervals for total (approximately −0.1% to +1.9%) and public (−1.8% to +2.2%) both include zero.

OBSERVATION: public construction remained stronger over longer nominal comparisons: +2.472% year over year and +1.150% for January–August cumulative not-seasonally-adjusted spending, while total cumulative construction was −3.088%. Yet public activity supplied only 0.0486 percentage points, or 5.69%, of the August total monthly increase. The public share of total construction fell from 25.031% to 24.867% as private activity increased faster.

INFERENCE: the release supports relative public resilience, not a claim that public work drove the August increase. Small positive education and highway point estimates do not establish breadth because their sampling intervals include zero. Material revisions also counsel restraint: the current vintage raised July public construction 0.622% relative to the prior retained vintage and changed the calculated June-to-July public movement from −0.246% to +0.075%.

ASSUMPTION: published within-release definitions are comparable enough for nominal ownership and category comparisons. No assumption converts SAAR levels into monthly cash outlays, real output, a federal-program effect, or an investment signal.

SPECULATION: linked federal authority, obligation, outlay and delivered-work records could eventually identify a policy channel. This report does not assign that channel a probability.

## Hypotheses and tests

The predeclared alternatives were:

- H1: public construction accelerated broadly and materially offset private weakness.
- H2: apparent public resilience reflects nominal composition, ordinary project timing or other non-federal mechanisms rather than a newly identifiable federal impulse.
- H3: a new federal cash-flow impulse is independently visible in period-compatible Treasury observations.

T1 — accounting and units: completed. Total equals private plus public exactly for August 2026, revised July 2026 and August 2025 in the retained cells. This checks table arithmetic, not survey truth.

T3 — composition: completed. Public's monthly contribution was +0.0486 percentage points versus +0.8054 points from private construction. Public accounted for 5.69% of the total monthly level increase, and its ownership share fell 0.164 percentage point. H1's material-offset prediction fails descriptively.

T4 — category breadth and uncertainty: completed. Public education rose 0.119% and highway/street rose 0.070% from revised July. The published rounded changes are +0.1% with 90% half-widths of 1.5 and 5.6 percentage points, respectively. Both intervals include zero. Positive point estimates are contrary evidence to a claim of uniform contraction, but they do not establish broad acceleration.

T5 — cumulative comparison: completed. Public January–August nominal NSA construction increased 1.150% from a year earlier; total construction declined 3.088%. This supports relative public resilience. It is not a quarterly growth rate, a price-adjusted measure or a federal contribution estimate.

T6 — revision sensitivity: completed. Relative to the September 30 retained vintage, the October release raised July total, private and public SAAR levels by 1.246%, 1.457% and 0.622%. June was revised up 0.907%, 1.110% and 0.299%. The implied public June-to-July direction changed sign. H1 is therefore revision-sensitive as well as statistically uncertain.

T7 — federal cash-flow sufficiency: blocked. The retained Treasury material defines modified-cash reporting but contains no compatible numerical receipts, outlays, balance or net-interest observations. H3 is not tested; no deficit or primary-balance number is inferred.

T8 — causal transmission: blocked. No price deflator, linked funding/project panel, exposure mapping, credible control pretrends, deduplicated procurement actions, deobligations or delivered-work evidence was available. H2 remains plausible rather than proven.

Transmission is descriptive: performed public construction can support contractors, materials and labor at project-specific lags. A named federal program requires a chain from legal authority through obligations and outlays to delivered work. Ownership classification alone cannot supply that chain.

Independent Fraction and Decimal calculations agreed for every central rate to more than 30 decimal places. This independently checks arithmetic on the same source cells; it is not independent economic confirmation.

## Limitations

One national monthly release supplies the new quantitative evidence. It is preliminary, nominal, seasonally adjusted at an annual rate and subject to sampling and nonsampling error. The retained extracts are selected cells from a connected-tool representation, not verified original publisher bytes or a historical release-vintage archive.

The point estimates are smaller than or close to their published sampling uncertainty. Later revisions may change both magnitudes and signs, as the July comparison demonstrates. The report lacks real-price adjustment, federal/state/local decomposition, project exposure, timing controls and a compatible cash-flow panel.

The current and prior reports share Census VIP/SOC ancestry. Any Housing or Industry report using the same release shares that underlying measurement and does not provide independent evidence. Treasury definitions add no numeric confirmation. No other final domain report was consulted before freezing these findings.

This is an exploratory national measurement exercise, not a forecast, causal estimate, security recommendation or claim that missing activity is zero.
