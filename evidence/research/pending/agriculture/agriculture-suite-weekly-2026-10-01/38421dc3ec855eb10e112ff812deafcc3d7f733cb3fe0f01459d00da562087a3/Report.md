## Hypotheses and tests

The primary competing explanations are production loss, stronger demand, and beginning-stock/use-accounting revisions. A like-for-like U.S. corn balance would require beginning stocks, production, imports, domestic use, exports, total use and ending stocks for each of two verified eligible release vintages, with the same marketing year and units. Those cells are absent. No stocks-to-use figure or balance decomposition was manufactured, and the balance calculator was not run on missing or synthetic inputs. The existence of an October report was not assumed from the calendar.

The fallback analysis is explicitly exploratory: retained values and some earlier arithmetic were visible before the plan was frozen. It examines local hydrological change, measurement consistency and eligibility for crop-loss inference. It is not an independent confirmation of earlier work.

**ASSUMPTION — rounding sensitivity.** Treating the last displayed digit as nearest-digit rounding gives newer-release change ranges of 7.675–7.785 ft for St John year earlier, 0.515–0.625 ft for St Thomas prior week, and 3.210–3.230 ft for St Thomas year earlier. Their signs survive this limited sensitivity. These ranges are not confidence intervals and do not model instrument error, measurement time or pumping.

**INFERENCE — scope-limited hydrological signal.** The newer point comparisons are consistent with lower local groundwater levels relative to their stated baselines. Rainfall, pumping, aquifer behavior, timing and measurement changes remain plausible contributors. No causal attribution among them was executed.

T4, the two-vintage commodity balance, is blocked. T5's coverage audit confirms that crop-stage weather attribution, disease-to-output attribution and fertilizer/shipment constraints lack their required exposure, outcome and control data. These are unperformed causal tests, not clean negative results. Crop acreage weights, irrigation, phenological timing, matched regions, harvest outcomes and revision histories are necessary to test an agricultural consequence.

**SPECULATION — economic transmission.** Persistently low groundwater could affect agriculture where farms actually depend on the measured aquifer during a vulnerable stage. That exposure is unmeasured here. No magnitude or direction is assigned to national food supply, retail inflation, company earnings or stock returns.
