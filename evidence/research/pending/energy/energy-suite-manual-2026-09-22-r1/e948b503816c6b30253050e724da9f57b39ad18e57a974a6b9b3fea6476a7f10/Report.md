# Energy: mixed stock comparators and a petroleum vintage check

## Summary

Gas stocks rose during the measured week and remain above the five-year average, despite being below the prior year. Reused petroleum data show lower product stocks alongside weekly builds and strong refining. These mixed signals do not identify broad scarcity or demand growth. Electricity activity remains untested.

## Coverage

The new gas table covers September 11 versus September 4, released September 17. The electricity index identifies June 2026 data released August 26; its numerical tables were not acquired. A verified preexisting petroleum bridge contains 12 current-vintage weekly series through September 4, first observed September 14. This run selected 96 observations for new comparisons. A separately verified prior Energy final report covers petroleum through September 11. No petroleum feed was collected again.

## Hypotheses and tests

The selected leads were gas-storage imbalance, faithful petroleum reuse and electricity activity. Lower48 gas stocks were 3,298 Bcf versus 3,254 Bcf: +44 Bcf, matching the stated implied flow. Stocks were 122 Bcf (3.567%) below yearago and 118 Bcf (3.711%) above the five-year average. Top-level regional current/prior sums reconcile; five-year components differ from the national total by 1 Bcf, compatible with independent rounding. Salt/nonsalt are nested components, not extra regions. Net-change sampling standard error is 0.9 Bcf. These checks support the physical stock arithmetic, but do not establish unusual consumption, weather causation or scarcity without flow-seasonality and exposure controls.

The bridge's September 4 gasoline/distillate inventories were 5.936% / 11.907% below their 52-week comparators but rose 1,269 / 2,087 thousand barrels from the preceding week. Four-week year-over-year product-supplied changes were −1.406% gasoline, −2.577% distillate and −2.300% jet, whereas latest-week changes were positive. Window choice matters. Refinery input was 4.567% above its52-week comparator. These measures are related balance components and flow proxies, not independent confirmations or measured end-user consumption. SPR is reported separately from commercial stocks.

The eligible September 21 prior report and all 16 manifest members were verified. Eleven of 12 cross-vintage latest-level differences matched its reported weekly changes. Production did not: 13,944 minus bridge 13,947 equals −3 thousand barrels/day, while the prior report states zero weekly change. This can reflect vintage revision or a prior calculation/extraction difference; the available evidence cannot resolve which. Neither version is overwritten. The bridge and prior report receive one shared EIA petroleum evidence contribution. Their differing actual observation windows remain explicit.

Electricity sector changes and weather-adjusted demand cannot be calculated from an index. Same-source follow-up was deferred under preserved source cooldowns. Independently frozen matched weather controls were unavailable to this investigation; cross-domain causation remains untested.

## Limitations

All interpretations are exploratory. The bridge is older than the prior final report; neither represents a newly collected September 22 petroleum observation. Historical values are current-vintage, with unknown original release vintages. Raw petroleum bodies were not reparsed. Source estimates, reclassification and revisions matter; one zero gas reclassification difference is not proof none occur elsewhere. No price, global gas/LNG, hourly grid stress or industrial-demand coverage is inferred.
