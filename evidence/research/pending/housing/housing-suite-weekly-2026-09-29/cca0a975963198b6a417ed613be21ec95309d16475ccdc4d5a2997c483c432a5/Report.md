## Hypotheses and tests

### H1: Weakening sales absorption raises new-home months supply

**Executed descriptive test, supported with limits; persistent demand deterioration inconclusive.** Retained SA sales move from 678,000 SAAR in June to 607,000 in July (−10.47% using rounded levels). End-month for-sale stock moves from 479,000 to 488,000 (+1.88%). Recomputing months supply as `12 × stock / annualized sales` gives 8.478 and 9.647 months, consistent after rounding with the retained published 8.5 and 9.6.

The log change identity separates a 0.01861 inventory contribution and a 0.11062 falling-sales-denominator contribution. The latter represents approximately **85.6% of the computed log increase**. This is an accounting allocation using estimates, not a causal attribution of weaker demand. Simultaneous stock and sales covariance and nonlinear rounding are not modeled.

The published July sales change of −10.5% with a 14.0-percentage-point 90% margin spans **−24.5% to +3.5%**, including zero. The stock change of +1.9% with a 1.2-point margin spans +0.7% to +3.1%. Rising stock does not identify why sales moved. Completions, mix, seasonal adjustment and revisions remain alternatives.

February–April versus May–July mean SAAR sales move from 643.33 thousand to 638.33 thousand, **−0.78%**. An exploratory sensitivity removing the last month from each block gives **+1.47%**. This direction reversal weakens a persistent sales-decline narrative. There is no 36-month baseline or same-month year-earlier control, so the full financing-demand recipe remains incomplete.

### H2: Broad construction pipeline deterioration

**Broad claim weakened by composition; full mechanism inconclusive.** March–May versus June–August mean total permits move from 1,398.67 thousand to 1,400.33 thousand SAAR (**+0.12%**). Single-family permits decline **0.90%**, while five-plus permits rise **1.45%**. Total permits remain mildly positive (+0.75%) in the exploratory two-month-block sensitivity. Five-plus is not all multifamily: two-to-four-unit buildings are a separate part of the total.

July–August total starts move from 1,309,000 to 1,275,000 SAAR (**−2.60%**), while single-family starts rise from 853,000 to 918,000 (**+7.62%**). The total-minus-single-family residual declines from 456,000 to 357,000 (**−21.71%**); it is the non-single-family residual, not specifically the five-plus series. No residual sampling interval is inferred without covariance.

The retained 90% intervals for reported changes are −14.6% to +9.4% for total starts and −6.4% to +21.6% for single-family starts. Both include zero. Total completions fall from 1,280,000 to 1,128,000 (**−11.88%**) and single-family completions from 911,000 to 816,000 (**−10.43%**). Their retained change intervals, −21.6% to −2.2% and −19.7% to −1.1%, exclude zero under the publisher's stated convention. No multiple-comparison correction was performed; these are not calibrated probabilities of a recession or bottleneck.

Fewer completions can reflect earlier starts, mix, weather or normal project timing. Permits are authorizations, starts are work begun and completions are finished units. Differences between them are not automatically a measured backlog or duration. Absent under-construction stocks, compatible 3/12-month flows and labor/material controls, the demand-versus-completion-constraint test remains blocked.

### H3: Financing, labor or CRE stress explains the pattern

**Untested causal claim.** The September 17 PMMS term spread is 69 basis points, but the products differ in maturity and borrower composition. That cross-product spread is not a weekly rate increase. One week's rate observations cannot explain earlier July sales or establish a 0–3-month lag relationship. Applications, household controls and existing-home lock-in effects are unavailable.

No new BLS construction evidence was verified after the acquisition interruption; SLOOS was not acquired after the pass closed. Survey-only bank caution would in any event be insufficient to establish realized CRE losses or physical construction effects. No quantitative labor/material, refinancing or property-outcome transmission finding is claimed.

## Limitations

The scientific result is a bounded, partly executed hypothesis investigation. Current-source collection did not succeed, and reusable cells may be stale or revised. The extraction metadata use some broad shared universe descriptions; calculations here restrict each row to its named source, structure type, period and stock/flow unit. Percent changes use the earlier positive level as denominator; seasonal status is retained. Unknown values remain missing. There is no full 36-month history, NSA control, income/borrower composition control, weather exposure test, listing panel, material quantity series, measured build time or realized CRE outcome. The selected short windows and sensitivities are exploratory. Evidence access readiness is not successful fresh acquisition.
