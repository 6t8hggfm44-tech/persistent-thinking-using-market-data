# Freight: near-flat port totals conceal lower loaded traffic

## Summary

OBSERVATION — Retained Port of Los Angeles figures show **August 2026 loaded traffic down 2.54% year-on-year**, while empty-container traffic rose **4.16%**. Total TEUs fell only **0.26%**. Rising empties offset **84.72%** of the loaded decline, so the nearly flat headline does not describe unchanged cargo-bearing throughput.

INFERENCE — This supports a narrow local composition finding. It does **not** establish weaker nationwide freight demand, a transport bottleneck, higher freight prices or lower carrier profits. Rail, national freight-index and grain-corridor tests remain unperformed because their retained records contain no usable numerical tables.

This September 23 report reuses ten cells retrieved September 22; it adds **no new publisher observation**. The same Port LA measurement appears in Trade and earlier manual Freight reports and must receive only one evidentiary contribution. Attribution: [Port of Los Angeles container statistics](https://portoflosangeles.org/business/statistics/container-statistics).

## Hypotheses and tests

The plan assessed three leads before current calculations: loaded-versus-empty composition, cross-mode concordance, and one grain-corridor price/quantity case. Only the first had compatible numerical inputs. Prior exposure is explicit: this conversation already contained Trade's Port LA result. This is exploratory reanalysis, not a blinded independent confirmation or a prospective forecast.

**H1 — Aggregate port activity can mask loaded/empty composition. Supported locally.** T1 recomputed loaded imports plus exports and then loaded plus empty containers. Both identities have zero residual at the displayed fractional-TEU precision in both years. Loaded traffic fell 16,030.25 TEUs, but empties rose 13,581.60, leaving a total decline of 2,448.65.

Loaded traffic's share fell from **65.94% to 64.43%**, a **1.51-percentage-point** reduction. Relative to the August 2025 total, imports contributed −0.4396 percentage points, exports −1.2331, and empties +1.4172, summing to −0.2555%. Lower loaded traffic is not simply an empty-only decline; increasing empties are the contrary evidence that makes the overall total look more stable.

T4 separately parsed the retained display strings using exact rational arithmetic and checked the Decimal growth rates, offset ratio and component contributions to 12 decimal places. This is an independent calculation method on the same evidence, not a second source. No input imputation, calibrated anomaly threshold or causal probability was used.

**H2 — Freight movement changed broadly across modes. Untested.** T2 requires compatible BTS and rail observations, exact event months, revisions and mode ancestry. No such numerical tables were retained. An index title cannot substitute for a level, and weekly rail counts cannot be assigned to months without the publisher's transformation. BTS includes AAR and other mode inputs; their agreement would not automatically be independent evidence.

**H3 — A corridor bottleneck raised transport costs while constraining throughput. Untested.** T3 lacks a same-corridor rate/quantity pair, rate basis, valid fuel decomposition, unaffected control and dated operational event. USDA index availability alone establishes none of these. Grain rates expressed as a percentage of tariff cannot be treated as dollars per ton, and a price increase alone would not identify a bottleneck.

T5, the multimonth, revision and comparator sensitivity, is blocked. There is no YTD/time-series panel, earlier vintage, alternate-port series or cargo-mix adjustment in the retained input set. This prevents a persistence, seasonally adjusted trend or unusual-tail conclusion.

## Limitations

The strongest observed contrast is lower loaded traffic alongside higher empties. It warrants separating those series, not choosing a macroeconomic explanation. Plausible alternatives include port substitution, empty repositioning, inventory/front-loading, tariff timing, terminal coverage, capacity and calendar/cargo-mix changes. No matched operational or comparator data discriminate among them. Missing locations are not unaffected controls.

ASSUMPTION — The earlier semantic review correctly associated the retained values with the publisher's August row and year columns. Exact checkpoint and prior-package hashes verify the retained record, not original publisher-body identity or unseen revisions. Publication timing remains unknown. A more complete table could change the interpretation.

INFERENCE — Loaded handling exposure at this port decreased. Effects on local truck/rail transfers depend on modal shares and timing; effects on carrier revenue also depend on rates, mix and contracts. SPECULATION — Broader demand weakness or routing changes could explain the local pattern, but neither is demonstrated. No fuel-cost, inflation, earnings or asset-price effect is claimed.
