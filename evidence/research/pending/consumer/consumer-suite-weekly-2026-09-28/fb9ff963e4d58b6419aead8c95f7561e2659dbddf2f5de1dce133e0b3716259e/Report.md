## Hypotheses and tests

The plan was recorded before recalculation. Prior Consumer inputs/results and earlier Credit/Labor conclusions had already been seen, so the exercise is exploratory. No counterpart numeric inputs, market prices or subsequent outcomes were used to select a favorable result.

**H1 — nominal spending strength reflects stronger real purchasing power: untested.**

**OBSERVATION:** Retained total retail and food-service sales were $768,587 million in June, $764,462 million in July and $773,947 million in August. Recomputed nominal changes are −0.537% in July and +1.241% in August; August was +0.697% above June. Excluding auto and gasoline categories, the corresponding levels are $563,861 million, $562,442 million and $569,265 million, giving −0.252%, +1.213% and +0.958%, respectively. The exclusion group is nested in the total and is not independent corroboration.

The separately retained August headline is +1.2% with a ±0.4 percentage-point 90% sampling margin, yielding a reported interval of +0.8% to +1.6%. This supports positive nominal growth within that published sampling framework; it does not cover every revision or nonsampling error. July's reported decline changed from 0.6% to 0.5% in magnitude, a +0.1-point revision. The table calculation rounds to the revised −0.5% headline.

**INFERENCE:** A nominal rebound survives comparison with the June level and exclusion of auto/gas categories, but is not a real-volume result. Missing matched nominal PCE, real PCE, price index and real disposable income prevent the prescribed price-volume identity test. BEA's two discovery values cannot supply those missing definitions. No real-growth figure is invented by deflating retail with an unrelated price measure.

**H2 — borrowing composition corroborates broad spending growth or a cash-flow constraint: financing increase observed; broader claim untested.**

**OBSERVATION:** G.19 total credit outstanding rose from $5,153.6 billion in May to $5,168.1 billion in June and $5,186.2 billion in July. Revolving plus nonrevolving components reconcile to each total exactly at displayed precision. June increases were $6.7 billion revolving and $7.8 billion nonrevolving, totaling $14.5 billion. July increases were $2.8 billion and $15.3 billion, totaling $18.1 billion. Nonrevolving credit supplied approximately 84.5% of July's stock increase; revolving growth slowed relative to June even as total growth increased.

July's published annual-rate flows are $216.7 billion total, $33.6 billion revolving and $183.1 billion nonrevolving. Dividing by 12 gives approximately $18.058 billion, $2.800 billion and $15.258 billion. Differences from observed monthly stock changes are approximately $0.042 billion, zero and $0.042 billion. These residuals fit the scale of displayed rounding; they do not prove the absence of source adjustments. The stock difference does not replace the published adjusted flow. Retained simple annualized growth rates of 4.2%, 2.5% and 4.8% are annual rates, not monthly growth percentages.

**INFERENCE:** Credit rose in both categories, with a pronounced shift toward nonrevolving growth. G.19 is not card-purchase volume, total household debt or a delinquency measure. July retail fell while July credit increased; that descriptive divergence cautions against treating borrowing as a spending proxy, but does not identify who borrowed or why. Missing contemporaneous real income/PCE and delinquency denominators block a financial-distress conclusion. Most real-estate-secured credit is outside G.19's scope.

**H3 — a behavioral proxy corroborates consumption: blocked.** TSA is disabled, with no complete seven-day counts, weekday/holiday controls or verified substitute. Card panels, hotel occupancy, airline bookings, physical vehicle sales and search/activity proxies are unavailable. Restaurant sales dollars cannot be relabeled as restaurant visits, and vehicle-dealer dollars cannot be relabeled as vehicles sold.

## Limitations

Exact retained bytes establish extract identity, not original publisher-body identity, current availability or a full historical release archive. Generic extraction metadata and current-vintage selected cells limit detailed interpretation. Missing values were not filled. No complete category breadth, borrower-level exposure, survey covariance, household distribution or causal control was acquired.

**ASSUMPTION:** Displayed rounding provides the limited precision context for arithmetic comparisons, not a probabilistic model. **SPECULATION:** Changes in financing composition could affect later discretionary spending, but credit availability, repayments, auto/student composition, prices, category mix and revisions offer alternatives. No measured causal effect, distress probability, recession call or trading implication is established.

Independent arithmetic checks mapped inputs to exact retained locators, reconciled stock/flow components, recomputed ratios with rational arithmetic and checked retail headline rounding and revision direction. These are calculation audits, not new source confirmation. The earlier immutable report remains preserved; this report adds clearer period and measurement limits without changing its central arithmetic.
