# Geopolitical risk: events do not establish economic transmission

## Summary

Historical incidents and policy-update discovery are available, but the acquired evidence does not measure their effect on civilian trade, production, shipping costs or political-transition outcomes. This final evidence-gap conclusion preserves a valid incident-count arithmetic check and rejects unsupported economic attribution.

## Coverage

Four sources were reviewed on September 22: OFAC updates, BIS announcements, IMO's Red Sea aggregate incident page and International IDEA's tracker landing page. The IMO total has no explicit exact as-of date. None supplies the complete outcome/comparator data required by the registered transmission tests. No individual vessel, person or tactical analysis was performed.

## Hypotheses and tests

Three leads were selected: restriction transmission, civilian chokepoint effects and political transitions. OFAC's displayed September 14–18 entries mix additions, removals and licensing changes. A count cannot establish the direction of net restriction. BIS announcements similarly do not replace original legal instruments or exposed production/trade observations. Restriction transmission is untested; exemptions, timing, substitution and general demand remain alternatives.

The IMO page gives 61 cumulative confirmed/notified incidents since January 10, 2024, including 57 through December 31, 2025. Subtraction yields an implied residual of four after that boundary, conditional on consistent definitions. It is not a current-week count. The earlier count of 17 belongs to a separate pre-boundary reporting period. Adding the included 57 to 61 would double-count. No comparable exposure denominator or exact endpoint permits a rate comparison. Corridor throughput, costs, voyage duration, fuel and unaffected-route controls are absent, so economic transmission remains untested.

The IDEA landing page displays a December label without an unambiguous year and a sample record with placeholder content. It was excluded as evidence for current transition events. Country scores or headline frequency cannot stand in for a certified election, implemented policy or economic effect.

## Limitations

This is not a finding of normal geopolitical conditions or no economic effect. Notifications can change through reporting coverage and revisions; cumulative counts are not independent evidence votes. Announcements and implementation dates differ. The needed legal instruments, event-aligned economic series and controls were not acquired, and actual source cooldowns were preserved. Interpretation is exploratory, with no causal probability or market-response estimate.
