# Freight and supply chains — October 7, 2026

## Summary

**Final finding: measured freight activity weakened through mid-2026, but the evidence does not establish a current nationwide bottleneck.** The official BTS release index shows three consecutive monthly Freight TSI declines from May through July 2026: 1.3%, 0.3%, and 0.7%. The reported year-over-year rate moved from +1.4% in April to −2.0% in July. Compounding the rounded monthly headlines implies an approximate 2.28% decline from April to July.

**OBSERVATION:** BTS's national for-hire composite weakened across three months. Separately, August loaded containers at the Port of Los Angeles were 2.54% below a year earlier while empties rose 4.16%, leaving total TEUs nearly flat. **INFERENCE:** the BTS sequence is evidence of persistence over time inside a composite measure, and the port accounting shows that empty repositioning can conceal weaker loaded cargo locally. **It is not independent cross-mode confirmation:** BTS embeds modal sources including AAR rail, and the port uses a later month, a single geography, and a different transformation.

## Hypotheses and tests

The investigation predeclared three competing explanations before the authorized BTS command: broad freight-cycle weakening, port-specific composition/diversion, and a corridor capacity bottleneck. Tests preserve geography, period, units, revisions, and source ancestry.

### H1: Freight weakness persisted over multiple months

**Supported within the BTS composite, with important measurement limits.** [BTS](https://www.bts.gov/tags/transportation-services-index) reported monthly changes of +0.4% in March, +0.3% in April, −1.3% in May, −0.3% in June, and −0.7% in July 2026. Year-over-year changes were +0.7%, +1.4%, −0.3%, −1.7%, and −2.0%, respectively.

T1 found three consecutive negative monthly headlines from May through July. Multiplying the displayed rounded changes gives an approximate April-to-July decline of 2.28%; this is not a reconstructed official index. The year-over-year rate deteriorated 3.4 percentage points from April to July. A revision warning on the official index means these displayed values are current-vintage observations, not immutable historical releases.

### H2: Empty movements can mask weaker loaded cargo

**Supported descriptively at one port.** Credit: [Port of Los Angeles](https://portoflosangeles.org/business/statistics/container-statistics).

| Component | August 2026 TEUs | August 2025 TEUs | Recomputed YoY |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Loaded imports | 500,301.50 | 504,514.25 | −0.84% |
| Loaded exports | 115,561.25 | 127,378.75 | −9.28% |
| Total loaded | 615,862.75 | 631,893.00 | −2.54% |
| Empty | 340,043.75 | 326,462.15 | +4.16% |
| Total | 955,906.50 | 958,355.15 | −0.26% |

T2 reconciled imports plus exports to loaded and loaded plus empty to total with zero residual in both years. Empty growth offset 84.72% of the loaded decline, and loaded share fell 1.51 percentage points. Excluding empties makes the growth rate 2.28 percentage points lower. These arithmetic results reuse observations already shared with Trade and earlier Freight work; they count once.

### H3: Weakness is broad across independent modes or caused by a bottleneck

**Not established.** T3 found no common observation month between the July BTS headline and the August port comparison. Their direction is consistent, but their universes, transformations, and geography differ. BTS incorporates AAR, ATA, air, pipeline, and waterborne inputs, so those ancestors cannot be counted again as independent confirmation.

T4 remains blocked because no same-corridor rate/quantity pair, fuel treatment, dated capacity restriction, or unaffected control was available. A higher rate by itself would not distinguish scarcity, fuel, mix, or demand. No disruption effect was estimated.

## Limitations

The newest numeric observation is August 2026 at one port; the national composite ends in July. BTS values were retained from an official connected-tool representation, not verified publisher-body bytes. They are rounded title values and may be revised. Full index levels, component weights, modal contributions, seasonal-adjustment details, publication timestamps, and vintage histories were unavailable.

The port comparison is year-over-year and unadjusted; BTS is a seasonally adjusted for-hire composite. Same-direction movement across disjoint months is not an independent replication. Routing changes, working-day effects, inventories, cargo mix, tariffs, and base effects remain contrary explanations. No conversion from TEU to tonnage, cargo value, margins, consumer demand, or inflation is made.

Central port arithmetic was independently recomputed with Decimal and exact rational methods. The BTS compound is only the mechanical product of rounded public changes. Computational agreement does not supply independent evidence or recover unavailable source precision.
