# Energy: gas deficit versus last year does not equal broad scarcity

## Summary

**October 8, 2026 — completed exploratory hypothesis tests with explicit coverage gaps.** The newest US natural-gas storage release is mixed: Lower-48 stocks are below last year but above the five-year average and within the historical range. Reused electricity evidence still shows commercial-led rather than industrial-led growth, while the verified petroleum bridge remains old and internally mixed. Together these observations do not establish broad physical energy scarcity, broad industrial acceleration or a market-direction signal.

OBSERVATION labels measured or recomputed results. INFERENCE labels interpretation. ASSUMPTION and SPECULATION are identified where relevant. No causal economic or investment-return model was executed.

## Hypotheses and tests

**Natural gas — accounting passes; broad scarcity is not established.** The [EIA Weekly Natural Gas Storage Report](https://ir.eia.gov/ngs/ngs.html) reports 3,500 Bcf for the week ended October 2 versus 3,415 Bcf a week earlier: an 85 Bcf injection. Reported implied flow also equals 85 Bcf. Stocks were 130 Bcf below the year-ago 3,630 Bcf, or −3.58%, but 68 Bcf above the 2021-25 five-year average of 3,432 Bcf, or +1.98%. EIA states the total remains within the five-year range.

Regional composition matters. South Central was 2.27% below its five-year comparator, while East, Midwest, Mountain and Pacific were all above theirs. The Lower-48 stock coefficient of variation was 0.4%, and the standard error for net change was 0.8 Bcf. INFERENCE: the release supports a year-ago deficit and localized South Central tightness, but contradicts an unqualified nationwide scarcity claim. Storage is not consumption, and missing weather, production, pipeline-flow and price controls block causal attribution.

**Petroleum — mixed signs and old endpoints reject broad current tightening.** All 16 bridge payloads matched their manifest, and all 3,420 rows were present with valid recorded hashes before calculations. The bridge ends September 4, so no present-week petroleum claim is possible. Exact weekly stock changes were commercial crude −391, gasoline +1,269 and distillate +2,087 thousand barrels. A synchronized inventory draw therefore fails.

Four-week means versus exact 364-day-shifted comparisons showed commercial crude stocks +1.30%, gasoline stocks −6.29%, distillate stocks −10.15%, refinery crude input +3.09%, gasoline product supplied −1.41%, distillate product supplied −2.58% and jet-fuel product supplied −2.30%. Refinery utilization averaged 97.6%, 2.5 percentage points above its comparator. INFERENCE: low product stocks can increase disruption exposure, but weaker product-supplied proxies, high refinery activity, trade, maintenance and seasonality remain competing explanations. Product supplied is a proxy, not measured final demand.

**Electricity — aggregate growth remains commercially led.** Reused [EIA Electric Power Monthly Table 5.1](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/epm_table_grapher.php?t=epmt_5_1) cells show July sales up 1.88% overall year over year: residential +0.99%, commercial +4.31%, transportation +3.97%, and industrial −0.26%. INFERENCE: broad industrial acceleration is not corroborated. Weather, billing timing, calendar effects, efficiency and customer classification remain uncontrolled alternatives. Monthly sales are not hourly load or total industrial energy use.

Across tests, OBSERVATION supports mixed and sector-specific pressure rather than one broad stress regime. ASSUMPTION: publisher labels and the reviewed tool representation correctly identify their tables; arithmetic checks cannot independently prove source truth. A claim that these data predict GDP, inflation, company earnings or asset prices would be SPECULATION.

## Limitations

This is exploratory analysis with prior exposure to earlier Energy results. US coverage is unevenly lagged: gas is current to October 2, electricity to July and the petroleum bridge to September 4. The petroleum histories are current-vintage evidence first observed September 14, not a historical release-vintage archive. They share EIA ancestry with original Energy and related work and count once.

No imputation was used. Stocks, flow rates, sales quantities and percentage points remain separate. Publisher estimates can be revised, and independently rounded regional gas values need not sum exactly. The connected response is a tool extract, not verified original publisher bytes; upstream request, byte and redirect totals are unexposed. Absence of tested broad stress is not proof of normal conditions.
