### H1 — Persistent weakening absorption

**Weakened, with uncertainty.** Current-vintage national new-single-family sales rise from 643,000 to 684,000 SAAR in July–August; end-month SA inventory remains 483,000. The monthly sales estimate has a 90% interval of −13.1% to +25.9%. The annual interval also includes zero; displayed NSA August sales are unchanged year over year. These controls do not establish a sustained rebound.

**Executed accounting test:** `12 × stock / annualized sales` yields 9.014 and 8.474 months, rounding to the reported 9.0 and 8.5. With displayed inventory fixed, the ratio's decline is arithmetically driven by its sales denominator. This allocation is not causal identification. Independently rounded components need not reproduce a published percentage change exactly.

**Executed revision audit:** July sales change from the earlier 607,000 to 643,000, a 5.93% revision; July inventory changes from 488,000 to 483,000 and months supply from 9.6 to 9.0. The fixed February–April versus May–July sales comparison changes from −0.78% to +1.09%. The current-vintage March–May versus June–August comparison is +3.25%; dropping the last month of each block leaves +1.15%. Short-window point estimates challenge persistent weakening but lack a long baseline and covariance-based uncertainty estimate.

The September 29 report accurately labeled its older vintage. Its frozen bytes remain intact: these are publisher revisions, not permission to overwrite historical findings or backdate new information. Completions, mix, seasonal adjustment and estimate uncertainty remain alternatives to a demand explanation.

### H2 — Broad construction deterioration or completion constraints

**Broad decline weakened; mechanism inconclusive.** Recomputed retained March–May versus June–August permits change +0.12% overall, −0.90% for single-family and +1.45% for five-plus buildings. Excluding the last month of each block preserves those directions. Five-plus is not all multifamily; two-to-four-unit buildings remain separate.

Retained July–August total starts fall 2.60%, while single-family starts rise 7.62%. Their respective reported 90% intervals, −14.6% to +9.4% and −6.4% to +21.6%, include zero. Total completions fall 11.88% and single-family completions 10.43%; retained change intervals exclude zero under the publisher convention. These are reused observations, without a successful new revision check.

A permit is authorization, a start is work begun and a completion is finished supply. Subtracting incompatible flows does not measure a backlog. Project mix, earlier starts, ordinary timing and weather could explain fewer completions. Missing stocks, labor/material controls and matching 3/12-month flows block the demand-versus-bottleneck test. No multiple-testing correction or recession probability is claimed.

### H3 — Financing costs explain activity

**Rate change observed; causal effect untested.** Retained September 24 PMMS rates are 7.03% for the 30-year fixed product and 6.42% for the 15-year product. Recomputed weekly changes are +8 and +16 basis points; displayed year-ago gaps are +73 and +93 basis points. Different products and borrowers cannot be treated as a matched panel.

These later rate observations cannot explain earlier monthly sales through a contemporaneous comparison. The full financing recipe needs a 36-month joint history, registered 0–3-month lags, applications volume and borrower/income controls. Existing-home lock-in effects are not measured. **ASSUMPTION:** Holding loan amount, term and borrower characteristics constant, higher rates raise financing costs; those controls have not been observed here. An activity forecast from this channel remains **SPECULATION** and is not adopted.

CRE transmission and price-cut tests also remain blocked. Lending opinions alone would not establish credit losses, vacancy or construction effects. A median price without composition controls would not establish a constant-quality price decline.
