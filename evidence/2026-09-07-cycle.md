## E-2026-09-07-01 — U.S.-Iran maritime escalation lifts oil to six-week highs

**Available:** 2026-09-05 through 2026-09-07; included by the 2026-09-07 17:28 ET cutoff  
**Sources:** Reuters  
**URLs:**

**OBSERVATION:** Reuters reported that U.S. forces struck three Iranian crude-oil carriers on Sept. 5 after Iranian missile attacks on U.S. Navy ships, with subsequent reciprocal threats and attacks around Gulf shipping. By Sept. 7, Reuters reported Brent crude near **$97.31/bbl** and WTI near **$92.65/bbl**, both at roughly six-week highs, while shipping through the Strait of Hormuz had slowed and Iran threatened additional restrictions and energy-infrastructure retaliation. Reuters also reported OPEC+ leaving October production levels unchanged.

**INFERENCE:** This is materially stronger evidence that the already-modeled geopolitical-energy shock has persisted and become more directly tied to physical shipping/supply risk. It supports H003's energy/input-cost/policy-constraint branch, but does not establish persistent core inflation, a Fed hike, or a recession. The shock also raises a later H002 pathway through real-income compression if it persists. Because the shock occurred in September, it is **not** evidence about the already-completed August core-CPI observation window for P000023; using it to reinterpret that forecast would be a timing/hindsight error.

**Expected under hypotheses:**
- **H001:** adverse but not decisive; a soft landing can survive a temporary oil shock if pass-through is contained and underlying inflation continues to cool.
- **H002:** ambiguous near term; higher energy costs can weaken household demand later, but no new U.S. labor/credit contraction evidence arrived in this holiday interval.
- **H003:** supportive; persistent physical energy-supply risk is directly consistent with the inflation/policy-constraint branch.
- **H004:** largely non-discriminating except through financing/input-cost headwinds.
