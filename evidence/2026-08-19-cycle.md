**Available:** 2026-08-19 14:00 ET release; Reuters coverage available before the cycle cutoff.

**OBSERVATION:** Minutes of the July 28-29 FOMC meeting showed that "several" participants were ready to raise the policy rate at that meeting and "many" judged that an increase would be needed if inflation failed to move toward 2%. The July meeting nevertheless held the target range at 3.50%-3.75%, with three dissents for a 25 bp hike.

**Sources:** Federal Reserve Aug. 19 calendar and July 29 statement; Reuters, Aug. 19, 2026: https://www.reuters.com/business/fed-policymakers-inflation-concerns-increased-july-meeting-minutes-show-2026-08-19/

**INFERENCE:** The minutes strengthen H003's policy-constraint branch: persistent inflation can generate renewed tightening even if growth softens. They are not a clean current-state policy signal because they describe the July information set and subsequent July CPI/PPI and employment data were softer.

**Affected hypotheses:** H003 positive at the margin; H001 negative at the margin because its benign path has less room for inflation persistence; H002 ambiguous because tighter policy raises downside risk but weak growth could itself reduce inflation pressure.

### E-2026-08-19-02 — Treasury doubles planned long-end liquidity-support buyback size

**Available:** 2026-08-19 before the cycle cutoff.

**OBSERVATION:** Treasury announced that the maximum size of long-duration nominal-coupon buyback operations in the 10-30 year sector will rise from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation for the Sep. 9-Nov. 4 period. Treasury's standing buyback framework describes liquidity-support operations as a way to improve secondary-market liquidity; its FAQ says the program is not intended as an acute-stress facility.

**Sources:** TreasuryDirect buyback rules/FAQ and Reuters/market coverage of the Aug. 19 special announcement. TreasuryDirect: https://www.treasurydirect.gov/laws-and-regulations/buyback-rules/ and https://www.treasurydirect.gov/help-center/faqs/buyback-faqs/

**INFERENCE:** Long yields now have another explicit causal input in the model: debt-management/liquidity-support policy can alter long-end liquidity and net duration absorption independently of contemporaneous growth or inflation news. The announcement itself is not proof that Treasury is targeting a particular yield level, and the buyback amounts remain small relative to the Treasury market.

**Affected hypotheses:** This does not map cleanly to one macro hypothesis. It potentially eases H003/H002 financial-condition transmission by lowering long yields, while also indicating that long-end market functioning/borrowing-cost pressure is policy-salient.

### E-2026-08-19-03 — Official Treasury curve falls sharply at the long end

**OBSERVATION:** Treasury's official CMT curve moved from **4.71% to 4.65%** at 10 years and from **5.28% to 5.19%** at 30 years between Aug. 18 and Aug. 19. The Aug. 19 rates are based on market quotations at or near 3:30 p.m. ET.

**Source:** U.S. Treasury Daily Treasury Rates: https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value=2026&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve

**INFERENCE:** The timing is consistent with the buyback announcement easing long-end yields, but the endpoint cannot identify the announcement as the sole cause. This is direct evidence for the need to separate policy/liquidity, inflation, growth, issuance, and term-premium channels when interpreting long rates.

**Affected hypotheses:** Marginally supportive of H001 through easier financial conditions; marginally negative to the strongest version of H003 that treats high long yields as mechanically persistent; neutral on the deeper fiscal/inflation hypothesis because policy intervention can mask the endpoint.
