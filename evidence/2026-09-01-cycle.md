## E2 — July JOLTS directly confirms low-hire / low-fire labor-flow asymmetry
**Available:** 2026-09-01 10:00 ET  
**Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey  
**URL:** https://www.bls.gov/jlt/

**OBSERVATION:** July job openings were **7.271 million**, up from a downward-revised 7.182 million in June. The openings rate was **4.4%**. Hires fell to **5.054 million** and the hires rate to **3.2%**. Quits fell to **3.056 million** / **1.9%**, while layoffs and discharges fell to **1.666 million** / **1.0%**. Reuters reported the June openings level had been revised down from 7.359 million and noted the JOLTS response rate is only a little above 30%, a data-quality caution.

**INFERENCE:** This is direct flow evidence for the model's Aug. 20 labor-flow asymmetry: hiring can weaken while layoffs remain low. It also reinforces the correction after P000020 that continued claims should not be used as a sufficient proxy for re-employment weakness. Weak hires/quits raise the probability of a cooling labor-demand regime, but low layoffs and still-large openings argue against declaring broad recession transmission. The evidence is therefore more discriminating than initial claims alone but still supports a bifurcated rather than collapsed labor market.

## Skeptic against pre-update leader H003
The strongest contrary case is that today's real-economy evidence is not a clean inflationary-growth story. ISM new orders and backlogs slowed; JOLTS hiring and quits weakened; construction and single-family activity contracted. The high ISM Prices index and higher oil/yields may be heavily supply/geopolitical rather than demand-driven, which can eventually damage real activity instead of validating a durable inflationary expansion. JOLTS also has a low survey response rate and a downward revision to June openings, so one release should not be overweighted.

The cleanest prospective failure condition for H003 remains a synchronized labor deterioration in the Sept. 4 Employment Situation followed by easing price pressure. Conversely, resilient employment plus persistent core inflation would discriminate more strongly for H003 than today's market-price endpoints.
