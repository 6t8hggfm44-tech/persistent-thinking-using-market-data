## INFERENCE

The market is simultaneously pricing resilient/rising equity earnings expectations and greater odds of monetary-policy restraint easing, while incoming labor data now points to a materially weaker growth tail. Inflation and long-yield pressure remain high enough that a clean “bad news = easier policy = higher equities” regime is fragile.

## Current regime

## Most important causal claims

1. Weakening labor data lowers the probability of additional near-term monetary tightening, supporting duration-sensitive equities **unless** inflation reaccelerates.
2. Long yields near 4.6% remain a meaningful valuation constraint; another inflation-driven yield shock would challenge expensive growth leadership.
3. Credit remains the key cross-check: a labor slowdown without sustained spread widening is more consistent with soft landing than recession.
4. Oil/geopolitical risk is an important inflation shock channel and can quickly reverse the favorable effect of weaker labor data.

## Three largest uncertainties

1. Whether the July jobs contraction is signal or statistical/seasonal noise.
2. Whether inflation is sticky enough to prevent policy easing despite labor weakness.
3. Whether current equity strength reflects durable earnings/productivity gains or momentum/positioning at stretched valuations.

## What would surprise the model?

- Persistent equity gains accompanied by rising long yields, widening credit spreads, and deteriorating earnings revisions.
- A rapid labor rebound without renewed inflation pressure.
- High-yield OAS remaining exceptionally tight through a sustained sequence of negative payroll prints.
